Principal Investigator

Johanna A. Harvey, PhD

Assistant Professor of Wildlife Disease Ecology (she/her) · Department of Natural Resources Science

Johanna is a broadly trained evolutionary ecologist working on wildlife disease ecology, immunogenetics, and conservation and management science. Her research addresses emergent pathogens such as highly pathogenic avian influenza alongside co-evolved pathogens including avian malaria parasites and vampire flies, with the goal of understanding how wild bird populations respond, and how that understanding can be applied to conservation.

She received her PhD in Wildlife and Fisheries Sciences from Texas A&M University, studying avian malaria diversification. She then held a postdoctoral position with Dr. Sarah Knutie at the University of Connecticut examining urbanization and the invasive parasite Philornis downsi in Darwin’s finches in the Galápagos, followed by a Gerstner Scholar Fellowship at the American Museum of Natural History studying avian malaria and range shifts across latitudes with Dr. Susan Perkins. Before joining URI she was a jointly appointed postdoctoral researcher at the University of Maryland and the USGS Eastern Ecological Science Center, within the Disease Decision Analysis and Research group with Drs. Diann Prosser and Jennifer Mullinax.

Johanna considers herself fortunate to have found science. As a young adult, mentors opened up a world she did not know existed; she now works to provide those same opportunities to others and to build a more inclusive environment in ecology and evolutionary biology.

Outside the lab: reading fantasy, hiking, gardening, textile crafts, swimming, and spending time with her partner and their golden retriever.

Researchers & staff

The people who keep the freezers cold, the samples labeled, and the questions moving forward.

Wenling (Abbey) Yang

Lab Manager (she/her)

I joined the Harvey Lab as Lab Manager with a background in Environment and Sustainability, concentrating on Ecology and Applied Biology. I oversee lab operations and support the lab’s research on avian disease ecology by optimizing assays and methods and by training and mentoring students. I am also working on an independent research project on differential RNA expression across host tissues in waterfowl encountering avian influenza. My interests are in how viral pathogens evade host immune defenses, and in molecular diagnostic assays for pathogen surveillance.

Outside of the lab: tennis, baking, Nintendo Switch games, and FaceTiming my cats.
